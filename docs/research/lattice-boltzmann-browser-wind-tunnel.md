# Boolean lattice gases grew into GPU wind tunnels

The search for Navier-Stokes in cellular automata effectively ended in 1992, when two groups published lattice BGK models; since then, researchers have refined the lattice Boltzmann method (LBM) into a production CFD tool, and it is now small enough to run in a browser tab. Four changes since the early 2000s matter most to someone returning to the field. First, a family of collision operators (TRT, regularized, central-moment, cumulant, entropic/KBC) now keeps coarse, high-Reynolds-number grids stable where plain BGK blows up. Second, mathematicians proved in 2022 that every lattice Boltzmann scheme is algebraically equivalent to a multi-step finite-difference scheme, which ends the old argument over whether LBM is "really" kinetic theory. Third, GPUs made LBM one of the fastest CFD methods available: a consumer RTX 4090 sustains about 11.5 billion D3Q19 cell updates per second, and a 2026 run on Aurora reached 21 trillion. Fourth, industry adopted LBM for automotive virtual wind tunnels and aeroacoustics. The history needs one correction. The lattice gas work was done at Los Alamos National Laboratory's Center for Nonlinear Studies (CNLS) under Gary Doolen. The Santa Fe Institute published the proceedings of the 1987 Los Alamos workshop in its book series, and the 1989 meeting was a NATO workshop held at CNLS. For the browser wind tunnel, WebGPU now ships by default in Chrome, Edge, Safari and Firefox, and a search turned up only hobby-grade WebGPU LBM demos. That gives a clear build: a D2Q9 fused stream-collide compute kernel with TRT and Smagorinsky, a ramped inlet, a sponge outlet, bounce-back obstacles with momentum-exchange forces, and validation against the Schäfer-Turek cylinder benchmark (c_D 5.57 to 5.59 at Re 20; St 0.295 to 0.305 at Re 100).

## Hexagons fixed isotropy in 1986; Boltzmann averaging removed the noise by 1992

The lineage starts with Hardy, Pomeau and de Pazzis. Their HPP model ([J. Math. Phys. 1973](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1666248); [Phys. Rev. A 1976](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.13.1949)) moved unit-speed Boolean particles along the four links of a square lattice. It conserved mass and momentum, but its rank-4 velocity tensor is anisotropic, so its vortices come out square ([Wikipedia: HPP model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPP_model)). Frisch, Hasslacher and Pomeau switched to a hexagonal lattice, whose six-fold symmetry makes the fourth-rank tensors isotropic, and recovered the Navier-Stokes equations in **Physical Review Letters in April 1986** ([PRL 56, 1505](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.56.1505)). Stephen Wolfram worked in parallel. While consulting at Thinking Machines in mid-1985, "partly in an attempt to find uses for the Connection Machine," he devised a cellular-automaton fluid scheme and analyzed it over the following winter ([Wolfram, NKS notes](https://www.wolframscience.com/nks/notes-2-3--my-work-on-cellular-automata/)). His Chapman-Enskog derivation produced "slightly modified Navier-Stokes equations" ([J. Stat. Phys. 45, 471](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01021083)). The modifications were a density-dependent factor g(ρ) ≠ 1 multiplying the advection term, which came from Fermi-like exclusion statistics, and a velocity-dependent pressure. Three dimensions required a detour: no 3D lattice with single-speed links has rank-4 isotropy, so d'Humières, Lallemand and Frisch projected the 24-link four-dimensional face-centered hypercube down to 3D ([Frisch et al., Complex Systems 1, 649](https://www.complex-systems.com/abstracts/v01_i04_a07/)).

Succi's 35-year retrospective gives the reasons lattice gases lost as a CFD method: statistical noise, low collision rates, and collision tables that grow as 2^b. For the FCHC lattice (b = 24) that means **"O(2^24 ~ 16 millions) boolean operations at each lattice site"**, and practical interest "started to flag down in the early 90's" ([Succi 2022, arXiv:2209.13436](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13436)). Orszag and Yakhot had already argued in 1986 that lattice-gas cost scales badly with Reynolds number ([PRL 56, 1691](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.56.1691)). Several steps between 1988 and 1992 repaired these problems. McNamara and Zanetti replaced Boolean occupation numbers with ensemble-averaged real distributions, which removed the noise while keeping the lattice-gas collision rule ([PRL 61, 2332](https://www.scholarpedia.org/article/Lattice_Boltzmann_Method)). In 1989, Higuera and Jiménez linearized the collision operator, and Higuera, Succi and Benzi designed the collision matrix directly ("enhanced collisions") instead of deriving it from Boolean rules ([Succi 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13436)). In 1992, Qian, d'Humières and Lallemand published a "noise-free" lattice BGK model with "Galilean invariance and velocity-independent pressure," and they introduced the DnQm naming ([EPL 17, 479](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1209/0295-5075/17/6/001)). Two months later, Chen, Chen and Matthaeus showed that FHP's non-Galilean invariance and velocity-dependent equation of state "could be eliminated exactly" ([Phys. Rev. A 45, R5339](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.45.R5339); [Chen & Doolen 1998](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.fluid.30.1.329)). Lattice BGK therefore has two parallel 1992 origins, one in Paris and one in the United States, and both deserve credit. The designed collision matrix of 1989 also means that multiple-relaxation-time thinking is about as old as BGK. MRT did not start as a later patch.

The Chapman-Enskog picture has held up. A Taylor expansion in the time step, combined with a double expansion in small Knudsen and Mach numbers, recovers weakly compressible Navier-Stokes from an equilibrium that truncates the Maxwellian at second order. As a result, Galilean invariance holds "only to a finite order in the Mach number" ([Succi 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13436)). Later work filled in the rigor that the formal multi-scale ansatz lacked. Junk, Klar and Luo replaced it with regular asymptotic analysis under diffusive scaling ([J. Comput. Phys. 210, 676](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2005.05.003)). Shan, Yuan and Chen recast lattice design as Hermite expansion plus Gauss quadrature ([J. Fluid Mech. 550, 413](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022112005008153)).

## Los Alamos did the science; the Santa Fe Institute published it

A common shorthand, "the 1989 SFI/Los Alamos lattice gas workshops," merges two separate events and credits the wrong institution. David Campbell, the founding director of CNLS, writes that FHP's insight, together with parallel computing, "launched a cottage industry at CNLS on these algorithms, evolving eventually into the extremely influential lattice-Boltzman computational fluid methods," and he names **Doolen (team leader), Hasslacher, Tsutomu Shimomura, Shiyi Chen and Hudong Chen** as the key players ([Campbell, Chaos 34, 042102 (2024)](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/cha/article/34/4/042102/3283545/The-center-for-nonlinear-studies-A-personal); quoted from a search excerpt because the full text returned an access error). Hasslacher, the "H" in FHP, spent more than twenty years in the LANL Theoretical Division ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brosl_Hasslacher)).

SFI appears in the record as a publisher. *Lattice Gas Methods for Partial Differential Equations*, edited by Doolen, is **Volume IV of the SFI Studies in the Sciences of Complexity** (Addison-Wesley, 1990). It covers "the directions that lattice gas research has taken from 1986 to early 1989" and is partly a reprint collection ([AbeBooks](https://www.abebooks.com/9780201156799/Lattice-Gas-Methods-Partial-Differential-0201156792/plp); [Routledge reissue](https://www.routledge.com/Lattice-Gas-Methods-For-Partial-Differential-Equations/Doolen/p/book/9780367152741)). Papers that cite the volume describe it as the proceedings of a workshop held in Los Alamos in August 1987 ([arXiv comp-gas/9403003](https://arxiv.org/pdf/comp-gas/9403003)). The exact workshop title has not been confirmed from the volume's front matter. The 1989 meeting was a **NATO Advanced Research Workshop at CNLS on September 6 to 8, 1989**. Its proceedings, *Lattice Gas Methods: Theory, Applications, and Hardware*, appeared as Physica D 47 (1991) and included "the lattice Boltzmann equation" as a major section ([Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Lattice_Gas_Methods.html?id=5HPrKf-nNuwC); [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/latticegasmethod0000unse)). No source found in this research says SFI hosted a lattice-gas workshop. The accurate attribution reads: Los Alamos CNLS for the people and meetings, Paris and Nice for FHP and much of the theory, Thinking Machines for Wolfram's parallel scheme, and SFI for the 1990 proceedings volume.

## Five things changed after an early-2000s thesis

### The collision operator became a design choice

Plain BGK has three known defects: an O(Ma²) compressibility error; a cubic velocity error in the viscous stress on standard lattices, because c_iα³ = c_iα on D2Q9 and the third moment cannot match Maxwell-Boltzmann; and instability as τ approaches 1/2. The instability comes from non-hydrodynamic "ghost" moments that relax at the same rate as the stress. The fixes of the past two decades all add control over those moments. Two-relaxation-time (TRT) models tie wall location to a "magic parameter" Λ ([Ginzburg et al., TRT stability](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10955-010-9969-9)). Regularized BGK filters the non-equilibrium part ([Latt & Chopard 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matcom.2006.05.017)), and recursive regularization improves it further ([Malaspinas 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.06900)). Central-moment ("cascaded") models relax in the fluid's frame ([Geier et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.73.066705)). Cumulant LBM targets low-viscosity stability ([Geier et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.camwa.2015.05.001)), and entropic KBC enforces an H-theorem ([Karlin, Bösch & Chikatamarla 2014](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.90.031302)). Coreixas, Chopard and Latt put all of these in one framework ([PRE 100, 033305](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12948)). Their follow-up is the most even-handed comparison available. It finds central-moment stability gains and "superiority of the tensor product basis compared to standard MRT," but notes that "it remains unclear if cumulant methods are always more stable than central moment LBMs" ([Coreixas et al. 2020, arXiv:2002.05265](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05265)). Work on TRT continues: a 2026 "purified TRT" removes ghost modes and was validated on Taylor-Green flow ([arXiv:2602.06686](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06686)). Succi notes that early hopes of ghost modes acting as a natural subgrid model "turned out not to be the case" ([Succi 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13436)). High-Re practice now pairs D3Q27 with cumulant or hybrid recursive regularized collisions, LES, and wall functions ([Kummerlaender et al. 2023](https://www.aimsciences.org//article/doi/10.3934/dcdss.2023212)).

### LBM is provably a finite-difference scheme

Bellotti, Graille and Massot showed that "any lattice Boltzmann scheme can be rewritten as a corresponding multi-step Finite Difference scheme on the conserved variables," which brings Lax-Richtmyer analysis to linear LB schemes ([Numer. Math. 152 (2022)](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00211-022-01302-2.pdf)). The non-conserved moments show up as extra time levels, so the initial non-equilibrium state acts as start-up data for the finite-difference scheme. That result explains why initialization and boundary closures affect accuracy as much as they do. Convergence proofs have advanced in step: Junk and Yang for periodic and some bounded domains ([Numer. Math. 2009](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00211-008-0196-0)), Bianchini and Natalini for vector-BGK convergence to incompressible Navier-Stokes in Sobolev spaces ([arXiv:1705.04026](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.04026)), and Simonis and Krause, who "unfold the discretization technique of lattice Boltzmann methods as chaining finite differences" ([arXiv:2208.06867](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.06867)). The proofs cover smooth solutions in simple domains. Nobody has proved convergence for the under-resolved, turbulent, complex-boundary regime where industry runs LBM.

### GPUs and 16-bit storage rewrote the performance budget

LBM is memory-bandwidth bound: FluidX3D reports 2.4 to 16.6 FLOPs per byte, so "performance is only limited by memory bandwidth" ([FluidX3D README](https://github.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D/blob/master/README.md)). Three techniques from the last decade exploit this. Structure-of-arrays layout lets GPU threads coalesce memory accesses. In-place streaming (the AA-pattern, Esoteric Twist, and Lehmann's Esoteric Pull) keeps a single copy of the distributions ([Lehmann 2022, Computation 10, 92](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3197/10/6/92)). Distributions stored in FP16 while arithmetic stays in FP32 "almost double performance, without impacting overall accuracy for most setups" ([Lehmann et al., PRE 106, 015308](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.08926)). Together these cut D3Q19 from **about 344 bytes per cell to 55**. The same README measures D3Q19 at **384 MLUPS on an 8-core Apple M1 GPU (758 with FP16 storage), 4,070 on an RTX 3060, and 11,496 on an RTX 4090** ([FluidX3D README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D/master/README.md)). At the top end, OpenLB reached 21,120 GLUPs on 4 trillion cells using 1,000 Aurora nodes in 2026 ([OpenLB performance](https://www.openlb.net/performance/)). FluidX3D simulated 40 billion cells of a Concorde landing on 32 MI210 GPUs in 33 hours ([GigaIO](https://gigaio.com/2023/08/32-amd-mi210-gpus-render-massive-simulation-33-hours-for-one-second-of-concorde-landing/)). The open-source field has shifted from Sailfish (2013) to FluidX3D (which is source-available and free only for non-commercial use), OpenLB, waLBerla with lbmpy code generation, GPU Palabos ([arXiv:2506.09242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09242)), and Autodesk's differentiable, JAX- and Warp-based XLB ([GitHub XLB](https://github.com/Autodesk/XLB)).

### Industry adopted the virtual wind tunnel

Dassault Systèmes bought Exa, the maker of PowerFLOW, in November 2017 ([Wikipedia: Exa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exa_Corporation)). PowerFLOW uses an LBM-VLES formulation with wall functions ([review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/300806731_The_Lattice-Boltzmann-VLES_Method_for_Automotive_Fluid_Dynamics_Simulation_a_Review)), and quieter electric drivetrains have made its wind-noise capability a selling point ([SAE/Mobility Engineering](https://www.mobilityengineeringtech.com/component/content/article/43539-sae-ma-02850)). Altair's GPU-native ultraFluidX promises "overnight transient simulations of full vehicle aerodynamics on a single server" ([Altair docs](https://2021.help.altair.com/2021.2/vwt/topics/getting_started/overview_ultrafluidx_r.htm)). In aerospace, LBM entries have run in the AIAA High-Lift Prediction Workshops ([AIAA 2022](https://arc.aiaa.org/doi/10.2514/6.2022-3433)), and NASA reported LBM running **about 15x faster than higher-order Navier-Stokes methods at comparable accuracy** for airframe noise ([NASA@SC17](https://www.nas.nasa.gov/SC17/demos/demo4.html)). Compressible LBM now reaches transonic and supersonic airfoils on GPUs ([Coreixas & Latt 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04465)). No reliable market-share figure turned up.

### Lattice gases came back in two niches

The noise that McNamara and Zanetti removed in 1988 is useful again for fluctuating hydrodynamics. Wagner's group builds integer lattice gases whose sampling collisions reach "comparable computational efficiency to entropic lattice Boltzmann methods." The same group reports that these gases retain correlations that "prevent the existence of a well defined Boltzmann limit," which shows that LB's molecular-chaos closure drops physics the automaton kept ([Seekins & Wagner, arXiv:2504.18659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18659); [Blommel & Wagner 2018](https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.97.023310)). Quantum computing has also revived lattice gases, because Boolean streaming and collision map onto qubits and unitary gates while LBM's nonlinear collision does not. The Peking University QLBM uses an ensemble lattice-gas description to make collisions linear and validated it against DNS on vortex merging and decaying turbulence ([npj Quantum Information 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-025-01142-6)). Dozens of other 2023 to 2026 preprints exist, but their demonstrations run on classical simulators, and none shows practical quantum advantage. Machine learning has made more concrete progress: collision operators that embed conservation laws and symmetries beat unconstrained networks by several orders of magnitude in accuracy ([Corbetta et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06124)).

## WebGPU lifts the browser ceiling from 600x240 to millions of cells

The reference browser wind tunnel is still Dan Schroeder's plain-JavaScript D2Q9 at Weber State. It provides curl, speed and density views, drawable barriers, tracers, flowlines, a force readout and a point sensor, on grids **from 60x24 up to 600x240**, and it describes itself as qualitative or semi-quantitative ([Weber State](https://physics.weber.edu/schroeder/fluids/)). On WebGPU, the most complete demo found (huj31415) adds a Schlieren view and image-to-obstacle upload, but it reports no performance numbers and offers no force readout ([huj31415](https://huj31415.github.io/lattice-boltzmann-webgpu/)). Other examples include a WebGL2 advection-diffusion LBM ([rafaelanderka](https://github.com/rafaelanderka/lattice-boltzmann-simulator)) and Dobryakov's popular WebGL stable-fluids solver. Dobryakov's code is a good reference for float-texture capability checks and ping-pong plumbing, but it contains no LBM physics ([PavelDoGreat](https://github.com/PavelDoGreat/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation)). FluidX3D has no web port.

WebGPU now ships by default in Chromium (v113+ on macOS, Windows and ChromeOS; Android 12+ from v121), Safari 26, and Firefox on Windows (v141) and Apple Silicon macOS. The remaining gaps are Linux outside Intel Gen12+ and recent NVIDIA on Wayland, Firefox on Linux and Android, and Chrome on Windows ARM64 ([gpuweb implementation status](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status)). **`shader-f16` appears on about 93% of WebGPU reports**, while subgroups are essentially Chromium-only (Firefox 0.48%, Safari 0.21%) ([Web3D Survey: f16](https://web3dsurvey.com/webgpu/features/shader-f16); [subgroups](https://web3dsurvey.com/webgpu/features/subgroups)). The spec's default limits are modest: 128 MiB per storage binding, 256 MiB per buffer, and 8 storage buffers per shader stage ([WebGPU spec](https://gpuweb.github.io/gpuweb/)). A 2D tunnel fits within them. A 3D mode has to request the adapter's higher limits.

Nobody has published a WebGPU LBM MLUPS figure, and this is the largest unknown. Two studies bound the answer. On a 2D stencil, native Dawn matched or beat Vulkan at good workgroup sizes, and the Emscripten browser build ran 13 to 20% slower than native Dawn, mostly because of buffer transfers ([Innsbruck thesis](https://ulb-dok.uibk.ac.at/ulbtirolhs/download/pdf/11304364)). A 2026 study measured per-dispatch overhead of **24 to 36 µs on Vulkan and 32 to 71 µs on Metal**, with kernel efficiency close to CUDA, and found that kernel fusion helps WebGPU by 53% on Vulkan ([arXiv:2604.02344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02344)). A rough estimate follows. D2Q9 moves about half the bytes per cell of D3Q19, so an M1 should reach roughly 800 D2Q9 MLUPS. At 60 fps that is about 13 million cell updates per frame, enough for a 1024x512 grid at about 25 steps per frame, with dispatch overhead near 1.25 ms of the 16.7 ms frame budget. Treat these numbers as a planning estimate until a benchmark confirms them.

## Recommendations for the browser wind tunnel, in priority order

| Priority | Decision | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Platform | WebGPU compute, one fused kernel per step, many steps per command buffer |
| 2 | Lattice and collision | D2Q9, TRT with Λ = 3/16, optional Smagorinsky; clamp τ ≥ 0.51 |
| 3 | Boundaries | Ramped equilibrium or Zou-He inlet; zero-gradient outlet with a 10 to 20% sponge; slip or periodic side walls in free-flight mode |
| 4 | Obstacles and forces | Halfway bounce-back mask with momentum exchange fused into streaming; Bouzidi links for SDF shapes |
| 5 | Validation | Poiseuille, Taylor-Green, Schäfer-Turek 2D-1 and 2D-2, Ghia cavity, NACA0012 |
| 6 | Visualization | Curl (diverging map), speed, Schlieren, GPU tracers; LIC later |
| 7 | Later | FP16 storage and Esoteric Pull, D3Q19/D3Q27 3D mode, cumulant or regularized collision, WebGL2 fallback |

**Technology.** Build on WebGPU and skip WebGL2 at first. Add a WebGL2 fallback, with nine distributions packed into three RGBA32F render targets, only if Linux Firefox or Windows-on-ARM users matter. Wasm SIMD is a last resort: 128-bit lanes give a few-fold speedup over scalar code ([Welihinda](https://www.awelm.com/posts/simd-web-assembly-experiment/)), and CPU memory bandwidth caps an LBM far below any GPU. The dispatch-overhead data calls for one fused stream-collide-boundary kernel per time step, with all steps for a frame encoded into one command buffer. Use a single SoA buffer indexed f[i·N + cell] to stay under the 8-binding limit, and tune workgroup sizes per device (64 to 256 threads, or 8x8 and 16x16 tiles), because the Innsbruck thesis saw large swings between sizes. For 2D, simple A/B ping-pong pull streaming works: a 2048x1024 grid needs about 75 MB per FP32 distribution copy. Save Esoteric Pull and FP16 storage for 3D, where D3Q27 in FP32 fits only about 107³ cells under the default binding limit. Treat subgroups as an optional speedup for force reductions, since Safari and Firefox lack them. Build a D2Q9 MLUPS benchmark first. It fills the biggest gap in the published record, and the results make good launch material.

**Lattice and collision operator.** Start with D2Q9. Every stability decision follows from Re = 3·u_lat·N/(τ − 1/2). At u_lat = 0.1 and τ = 0.51, a body 100 cells across reaches Re ≈ 3000, which is the practical edge for BGK. The Hou et al. cavity study ran unmodeled LBM only to about Re 10,000 ([arXiv comp-gas/9401004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/comp-gas/9401004)). Use TRT as the default: it costs little more than BGK, and **Λ = 3/16 makes the halfway-bounce-back wall position viscosity-independent** ([Ginzburg et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220513218_Viscosity_independent_numerical_errors_for_Lattice_Boltzmann_models_From_recurrence_equations_to_magic_collision_numbers)). Offer Smagorinsky as a local τ_eff computed in closed form from the non-equilibrium momentum flux ([lbmpy tutorial](https://pycodegen.pages.i10git.cs.fau.de/lbmpy/notebooks/06_tutorial_modifying_method_smagorinsky.html)), with C_s between 0.1 and 0.17. Hou et al. used C = C_s² from 0.0025 to 0.04 and found the best constant depends on geometry. Check the closed-form constant against Krüger et al. before shipping. Keep u_lat at or below 0.1 so that density variation stays near a few percent, since compressibility error scales as Ma². The UI should take Re and resolution as inputs, derive τ, and warn users when a requested Re would push τ below the clamp ([Barker parameterization](https://dbarker.uk/posts/lbm-parameterization/)). Hold cumulant or recursive regularized collisions in reserve for a later "high-Re" toggle. The Coreixas comparison gives no clear winner that would justify the extra code on day one.

**Boundary conditions.** Every open LBM boundary reflects some acoustic energy, because the method is weakly compressible. For the inlet, set the populations to equilibrium at (ρ = 1, u_in) for robustness during interaction, or use Zou-He velocity conditions for second-order accuracy ([Zou & He preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/comp-gas/9611001)). Either way, ramp u_in over a few thousand steps so the impulsive start does not set pressure waves bouncing between inlet and outlet. For the outlet, copy populations from the neighboring column (zero gradient) or apply a Zou-He pressure condition at ρ = 1. Add a sponge covering 10 to 20% of the domain length that raises viscosity smoothly, since an abrupt ramp itself reflects waves. With characteristic or standard boundaries plus a sponge, reflected kinetic energy and vorticity fell **below 0.1%** in vortex-convection tests ([Wissocq et al., arXiv:1701.07734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.07734)). Give users two tunnel modes: slip or periodic top and bottom walls for free-flight studies with low blockage, and no-slip walls with a parabolic inlet for the Schäfer-Turek validation.

**Obstacles and force measurement.** User-drawn shapes map to a solid mask with halfway bounce-back. The mask conserves mass exactly and is robust, but its staircase outline limits curved bodies to first-order accuracy. When shapes come from polygons or signed distance fields, compute the wall fraction q per link and apply Bouzidi interpolated bounce-back, which gives second-order accuracy for velocity, force and local stress ([Bouzidi et al. 2001](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/pof/article-pdf/13/11/3452/12713899/3452_1_online.pdf); [Peng, Ayala & Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05445)). Avoid the immersed boundary method for force readouts: it is first-order and underestimates interface stress. Compute forces by momentum exchange inside the streaming kernel. For each fluid cell and direction i whose neighbor is solid, add 2·c_i·f_i* for a stationary wall, then sum with a parallel reduction ([Wen et al., arXiv:1303.0625](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.0625)). Report C_D = 2F_x/(ρ₀·u²·D) and C_L the same way, and take St from zero crossings or an FFT of C_L(t) once transients have passed. Average C_D over a full shedding period, because staircase geometry adds noise. If users drag obstacles during a run, switch to Wen's Galilean-invariant momentum exchange, which uses velocities relative to the wall, and refill newly uncovered fluid cells from neighboring fluid values, since poor refilling causes most of the residual force noise.

**Visualization.** Match Schroeder's feature set first: curl with a diverging colormap, speed with a sequential perceptual map such as viridis, density, and Schlieren from the density gradient. Compute these in the fragment shader straight from the simulation buffer, so they need no extra storage. Add GPU tracer particles that are advected by the velocity field and faded into a trail texture, following the Mapbox wind-map technique ([Mapbox](https://blog.mapbox.com/how-i-built-a-wind-map-with-webgl-b63022b5537f)). Add streaklines seeded at fixed points, a live C_D/C_L trace, and a point probe. Line integral convolution comes next. It needs about 20 to 40 samples per pixel, and no browser LBM project was found that implements it.

**Validation benchmarks.** Run these as automated tests in the browser build. Before quoting the unconfined-cylinder and NACA figures, check them against their primary sources.

Schäfer-Turek 2D-2 costs little to run. With D = 20 cells, the grid is 440x82. A peak inlet speed u_lat = 0.1 (mean 0.0667) gives ν_lat = 0.0133 and τ = 0.54, which is comfortably stable, and halfway bounce-back should land within a few percent of c_D,max. The confined St of about 0.30 differs from the unconfined 0.164, and both differ from the loosely quoted 0.2, so every test has to compare against its matching configuration.

Two-dimensional results stay quantitative only for laminar flows. 2D turbulence runs an inverse energy cascade, so large vortices stay coherent where 3D vortex stretching would break them up ([Boffetta & Ecke 2012](https://courses.physics.ucsd.edu/2019/Winter/physics116_216/annurev-fluid-120710-101240.pdf)). Even at Re 20, the 3D Schäfer-Turek square channel gives c_D of 6.05 to 6.25 against 5.57 to 5.59 in 2D. The UI should label high-Re 2D runs as qualitative and present Smagorinsky in 2D as a stabilizer. It does not model real turbulence.

## Conclusion

Most of the progress since the early 2000s sits in the collision operator and the memory system. The Chapman-Enskog core is unchanged: the 1992 BGK equation still recovers Navier-Stokes the same way. What changed is that practitioners now choose how ghost moments relax, store distributions in 16 bits, and stream in place, and those choices, more than any new physics, turned a thesis-scale method into an overnight full-car wind tunnel. The 2022 finite-difference equivalence gives a returning reader a useful lens: boundary conditions, initialization and relaxation of non-hydrodynamic moments are where accuracy is won or lost, and that is where the browser tunnel's engineering effort should go.

A browser project also has a gap to fill. Schroeder's 600x240 CPU tool remains the standard for teaching, the WebGPU demos are unbenchmarked toys, and no one has published WebGPU LBM throughput. A D2Q9 TRT tunnel that shows live C_D, C_L and St, reproduces Schäfer-Turek inside the published bounds, and reports its own MLUPS across devices would be the first of its kind. It would also offer a direct way to see how far the lattice gas idea from Los Alamos, which began as an attempt to find uses for the Connection Machine, has come on commodity hardware.
